## 4. Verified taught word forms and grammatical boundaries

| Canonical lemma | Taught form | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|---|
| kahvi | **kahvia** | Taught partitive in Haluan kahvia; not a second lemma |
| tee | **teetä** | Partitive is not just tee + a |
| vesi | **vettä** | Stem change; `vesia` or `vesitä` not accepted |
| maito | **maitoa** | Unspecified milk amount/request |
| leipä | **leipää** | Correct vowel harmony and long vowel; not `leipa` |
| ruoka | **ruokaa** | Taught partitive of food |
| lounas | **lounasta** | Taught fixed partitive; independent full declension deferred |
| omena | **omenaa** | Taught partitive used after kaksi; not `omenat` |
| euro | **euroa** | **yksi euro** vs **kaksi euroa**, not `kaksi eurot` |
| kortti | **kortilla** | Fixed card-payment form; no productive adessive -lla/-llä paradigm |
| haluta | **haluaisin** | Polite conditional-shaped chunk; no conditional conjugation |
| ostaa | **ostan** | First-person buying; F12 tests Ostan maitoa |
| maksaa | **maksan** / **Se maksaa …** | Pay versus cost determined by sentence context |
| saada | **Voinko saada vettä?** | Fixed PhraseItem; preexisting Unit 3 recognition-only lemma identity retained |

**Partitive guidance:** Finnish frequently uses partitive for an unspecified amount of drink/food and in certain quantity/number expressions. It is **not** a word meaning ‘some’ and **not** necessarily a bare dictionary form plus -a/-ä. For nouns with different stems, show the complete taught form before assessment. Uusi kielemme and Aalto treat the general system as more complex than this beginner selection.

## 7. Linguistic sources, cross-checks and limits

References confirm **specific linguistic facts**, not that the sources externally approved our original exercises:

1. [Aalto OpenLearning — Partitive: Summary](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/mod/book/view.php?chapterid=560&id=10293&lang=fi). Explains uses with food/drink quantities and numerals above one, alongside limited common suffix patterns.
2. [Yle Supisuomea — Food and the partitive](https://yle.fi/a/20-156324). Finnish teaching resource with water `vettä`, tea `teetä`, coffee `kahvia`, and a real food-ordering context.
3. [Uusi kielemme — The Partitive Case](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/finnish-cases/grammatical-cases/the-partitive-case-partitiivi). Confirms that one versus two takes different noun forms and partitive has more than one use.
4. [Uusi kielemme — Partitive objects](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-grammar/syntax/rections/so-called-partitive-verbs-not-always-partitive). Supports the difference between mass-noun partitive `Ostan leipää` and the more general object-case system (which remains deferred).
5. [Finnish S2 food/partitive guide](https://flumi.io/grammar/fi/partitiivi-ruoan-juoman-ja-maaran-ilmauksissa). Independent check of `kahvia/maitoa/leipää/teetä/vettä` and `yksi omena/kaksi omenaa`; not treated as an authoritative dictionary.
6. [Finnish shopping phrase catalogue](https://lymi.app/explore/travel-finnish). Independent attestation of `Paljonko tämä maksaa?` and `Voinko maksaa kortilla?` in practical travel/retail contexts.
7. [Vokri curated 2,000-lemma CSV](../vocabulary/finnish_2000_curated.csv) and [provenance](../vocabulary/README.md), original Git blob `81341de6828ec5ffa3b6bebfc828ac31bb275776`. This validates source IDs/ranks, not native-teacher certification of its AI-authored examples.

**Limits and careful choices:** The partitive forms used here are standard taught forms. The shorter classroom café examples are newly authored, and register/real-world dialogue variation has not been externally tested with Finnish-speaking teachers or beginners. A `kaksi kahvia` serving request could depend on a count-of-servings interpretation; Unit 5 instead uses `kaksi omenaa` and `kaksi euroa` to keep the taught number pattern unambiguous. No artificially precise prices with cents are required.
