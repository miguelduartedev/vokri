## 6. Ambiguity and acceptance checks

- **Deixis:** A10 and E09 explicitly state whether the object is being pointed out across the room or has already been discussed. Se can be grammatically possible in many contexts; 'best fits the explicit task' is not a claim that every other form is generally ungrammatical. Retest with novice learners to ensure wording communicates the intended contrast.
- **Pronouns:** hän has no masculine/feminine distinction. Casual se for people is described accurately as recognition, not a student spelling target. The lesson's prompts never require choosing between he and she as if hän contained gender.
- **Person identification:** Kuka hän on? is explicitly introduced as a fixed question before any token-building test; the third-person verb on is not independently scored.
- **Language and country forms:** suomi / Suomi are not interchangeable with the forms suomea / Suomessa. English glosses qualify dictionary form versus taught sentence form. No case-generation algorithm is introduced.
- **Questions:** E02 tests only the taught olet→oletko; E03/E14 test Puhutko suomea? (with the person of the question specified), not arbitrary -ko placement.
- **Negation:** E04 marks En puhun suomea incorrect. E05/E06/E15 list the correct alternatives with explicit pronoun omission. en puhun and en asun receive useful near-answer morphology feedback but never full credit.
- **Deterministic N1:** NFC + casefold/trim/whitespace/punctuation separators only; preserve ä/ö, long vowels and exact word boundaries. No AI or fuzzy evaluator. Empty inputs require text or Not sure.
- **Non-scoring context:** D07 and E16 are openly capability-only. They do not award success to every vocabulary item visible in a long introduction.
- **Matching:** A04 (three pairs), B03 (four), B11 (four), C02 (three), C10 (two) total 16, each with a distinct English counterpart; the optional Word Match four-pair session minimum does not apply to small lesson exercises.

## 7. Research and source limits

The following sources were checked to verify specific language facts, **not** to copy their examples into the lesson or claim they approved the newly authored Vokri exercises:

1. [Kotus / Kielitoimiston ohjepankki — tämä, tuo, se](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/pronominit-demonstratiivipronominit-tama-tuo-se-nama-nuo-ne-yleispiirteet/). Supports context- and participant-relative interpretation, not a universal one-English-word rule.
2. [Kotus — hän vai se](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/pronominit-han-vai-se/). Confirms standard hän/he, gender-neutral hän, and conversational se referring to people.
3. [Kotus — negative verb](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/konjunktiot-eika-ei-eika-ja-ei/). Supports en/et/ei as inflected negative forms and the difference from a one-word ei response.
4. [Kotus — question punctuation and forms](https://kielitoimistonohjepankki.fi/ohje/kysymysmerkki/). Confirms Finnish question-word and -ko/-kö forms; full suffix-placement theory is deliberately deferred.
5. [Taste of Finnish — KO/KÖ examples](https://tasteoffinnish.fi/grammar/). Pedagogical cross-check of person-marked verb + suffix, including onko/oletko/puhutko-type forms. Not normative on its own.
6. [Aalto OpenLearning — Miten menee?](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/mod/page/view.php?id=3391&lang=en). Independent university-supported beginner resource for a natural “How's it going?” question.
7. [Uusi kielemme — Miten menee?](https://uusikielemme.fi/finnish-vocabulary/vocabulary-phrases/mita-kuuluu-miten-menee-how-are-you-in-finnish). Supplemental S2 comparison of Miten menee? and its adverbial replies.
8. [Vokri curated Finnish CSV](../vocabulary/finnish_2000_curated.csv) and [source README](../vocabulary/README.md). Authoritative *project* rank/lemma references, not external native-speaker certification. Original CSV remains unchanged (Git blob `81341de6828ec5ffa3b6bebfc828ac31bb275776`).

**Material not independently verified with a human teacher:** the naturalness of every new microdialogue, all inflection simplifications for novices, the suitability of distractors for this particular audience and whether the 12–18 minute question lesson is comfortable. These are product-owner/editorial review decisions, not false assurances.
